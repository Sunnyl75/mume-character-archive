# Fragments and shatter: full-response check

The retained output does not support treating fragments as restricted to pounding
or excluding piercing. The comparison must preserve both attack-effectiveness
wording and the injury-result clause, alongside the complete original line.

## Separate parts of one response

In *Diamonium gets played with inside warrens*, dated 2 November 1997:

> `*Tergrim the Hobbit* barely pierces your left arm and shatters it.`

The following prompt shows the observer at `HP:Awful`; the intervening message
is `You wish that your wounds would stop BLEEDING so much!`.

Source: `Pasted text (4).txt`, line 609.
[Retained original URL](https://web.archive.org/web/20010708034728/http://www.mume.net:80/oldsite/others/diamonium2.txt)

A second example, *Mammoth_Priest*, dated 21 August 1998:

> `You lightly pierce *Mammoth the Orc*'s right leg and shatter it.`

The prompts immediately before and after show Mammoth at `Awful`.
Source: `Pasted text (2)(5).txt`, line 10846; original URL not recorded.

These examples are consistent with the researcher's explanation that the injury
clause relates to damage relative to remaining HP. The exact numeric thresholds
or formula cannot be established from these excerpts. `Barely`/`lightly` and
`shatter` must not be flattened into one severity scale.

## Scope of the check

The pilot database was compared by attack verb, perspective and source log.
The raw bodies of all 25 retained logical logs were then searched for fragment
and shatter endings, including lines without final punctuation. Speech quoting
these terms and subsequent webpage comments were excluded. No individual log
body checked contained both endings. This is a property of this sample, not
proof that they could never coexist.

Further extraction should retain attack verb, effectiveness wording, body part,
injury clause, observer perspective and nearby HP display separately. Current
snapshots already preserve the whole raw line and context; they do not yet
model all those components as separate reviewed fields. No parser, catalogue,
character data or public page was changed for this analysis.
